Computing a core committee

The algorithmic companion to Existence of the Core in Approval-Based Committee Elections (arXiv:2609.11912v2), and a faster variant of it.

Understanding the problem Why the core exists Computing one

1From existence to an algorithm

The Why the core exists tab shows that a core-stable committee always exists, by exhibiting an objective — harmonic entropy — whose maximisers must admit a valid payment certificate. That proof is already constructive in a useful way: it needs only local optimality. A committee that no single-candidate swap improves by more than

\[ \delta \;=\; q - \frac{n}{k+1} \;>\; 0 \]

must satisfy core+. So hill-climbing on the objective is enough, and that is the paper's algorithm. This page walks through it, and through a faster rearrangement of the same proof.

What to take away

The proof tells you not just that a good swap exists, but which one: the losing candidate that violates (P3). Following that hint turns a scan of \(k(m-k)\) neighbours into a single step, and a sharper accounting of the truncation error shrinks each linear program as well.

2The paper's local search

Start from any committee of size \(k\) and repeat: solve a linear program for the best payment system, then try every swap of one winner for one loser, solving another LP for each, and take any swap that improves the objective by at least \(\delta/2\). Stop when none does.

Correctness comes straight from the two halves of the existence proof. If some loser \(c\) holds a full quota of reserve, adding it gains at least \(q\); some member of the enlarged committee can then be dropped for at most \(n/(k+1)\); and \(q > n/(k+1)\), so that one swap gains at least \(\delta\). Contrapositively, when no swap gains enough, no loser holds a quota — which is exactly condition (P3), and the certificate is complete.

The cost is visible in the description: each round solves \(k(m-k)+1\) linear programs, and each one carries \(\Theta(k^3)\) water levels.

Why a linear program at all

Harmonic entropy is defined by an infinite series, but it has a linear description once truncated. Using representation (6) from the proof tab,

\[ F_L(x) \;=\; \sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\ell+1} - t_\ell(x)\Bigr), \]

and each water level \(t_\ell\) is itself the value of a small minimisation: \(t_\ell(x) = \min\{\,t \ge 0 : \sum_j (x_j - t)^+ \le \ell t\,\}\). Maximising \(\sum_i F_L\) over the payment polytope is therefore one linear program in the payments, the reserves, the levels \(t_{i\ell}\), and auxiliary variables for the positive parts.

3Letting the violation choose the swap

The scan is redundant. The proof already identifies a swap that works, so there is no reason to examine the others. The faster algorithm is:

  1. Optimise the payments for the current committee once.
  2. Check (P3) directly. If every loser holds less than \(q\) in reserve, the payment system in hand is a certificate — return it.
  3. Otherwise pick a violating loser \(c\), with \(\sum_{i \in N_c} r_i \ge q\).
  4. Add it and re-optimise on \(X = W \cup \{c\}\), breaking ties toward the smallest total reserve — the deletion lemma needs that.
  5. De-reserve, then delete the member collecting the least.

One payment optimisation per round instead of \(k(m-k)+1\). Nothing about the guarantee is weakened: the round still improves the objective by at least \(\delta/2\), because that is what the add-then-delete bound says.

The one-LP trick

Steps 1 and 4 look like two solves per round, and in a first version they were. They collapse into one by carrying the target. The addition bound says the enlarged committee admits a system worth at least \(V(W) + q\) — but we do not need to know \(V(W\cup\{c\})\), only that this value is reachable. So instead of maximising, the LP is asked a feasibility question: among payment systems for \(X\) whose objective is at least the carried target, minimise the total reserve. That single solve supplies both the progress guarantee and the tie-break the deletion step needs.

It also explains why the carried system need not itself be optimal for \(W\). The invariant is only a certified lower bound on the objective, passed from round to round and increased by at least \(\delta/2\) each time.

4De-reservation

The deletion half of the proof needs payments pushed as high as the cap allows. Immediately after the LP, some voters may still hold reserve while approving a winner that has not reached \(q\) — and then no useful bound on the cheapest deletion is available.

De-reservation fixes that by a continuous process. Every voter with reserve left who approves an unsaturated winner spends it on those winners at unit rate. A stage ends when a voter's reserve reaches zero, or when a winner reaches \(q\). At most \(n + |X|\) stages can occur, since each one exhausts a voter or saturates a candidate.

Afterwards, the candidate with the smallest total is cheap to delete: total payments are at most \(n\) spread over \(k+1\) candidates, so the minimum is at most \(n/(k+1) < q\). Being unsaturated, all of its supporters have zero reserve, so each of them can simply rename her payment to it as her new reserve — which keeps (P2) because de-reservation leaves that payment her largest.

5The truncation, and the square-root improvement

Truncating the series costs something. Because the water levels shift by one index under the Robin Hood operator — \(t_\ell(\rho x) = t_{\ell+1}(x)\) — the exact gain \(x_{\max}\) becomes

\[ F_L(\rho(x)) - F_L(x) \;=\; x_{\max} - t_L(x), \]

so each voter loses \(t_L(x)\) of the promised gain. Bounding \(t_L \le 1/(L+1)\) and summing over supporters needs \(L = \Theta(n/\delta)\), which at the Hare quota is \(\Theta(k^2)\).

The improvement is to add a correction that depends only on the committee, not on the payments — so it shifts the objective without moving its maximisers:

\[ C_L(W) \;=\; \sum_{i \in N} \bigl(H_{L + u_i(W)} - H_L\bigr). \]

Against the corrected potential \(V_L + C_L\), a voter whose distribution has \(d = u_i(W)+1\) coordinates loses only \(t_L(x) - 1/(L+d)\). Writing the maximiser in Lemma 5.1 as \(S^\*\) with \(s = |S^\*|\) and \(\sigma = \sum_{j \in S^\*} x_j \le 1\),

\[ t_L(x) - \frac{1}{L+d} \;\le\; \frac{1}{s+L} - \frac{1}{d+L} \;=\; \frac{d-s}{(s+L)(d+L)} \;\le\; \frac{d-1}{(L+1)^2} \;=\; \frac{u_i(W)}{(L+1)^2}. \]

Summing over the supporters of \(c\) bounds the whole addition loss by \(E(W)/(L+1)^2\) with \(E(W) = \sum_i u_i(W)\). Choosing

\[ L \;=\; \Bigl\lceil \sqrt{\tfrac{2U}{\delta}} \Bigr\rceil, \qquad U \ge E(W), \]

makes that loss below \(\delta/2\), so every round still makes real progress. At the Hare quota this is \(\Theta(k^{3/2})\) levels rather than \(\Theta(k^2)\) — and the correction never has to be computed, since it only appears in the proof.

6One free simplification

Every reserve is at most 1, so a candidate approved by fewer than \(q\) voters can never gather a quota of reserve — it is a harmless loser under any certificate. Two consequences, both exact rather than heuristic:

  • If at most \(k\) candidates have \(q\) or more approvers, elect all of them, fill the remaining seats arbitrarily, and hand every voter a reserve of 1 with no payments. That trivially satisfies (P1), (P2) and (P4), and (P3) holds because every loser is low-degree. No LP is needed at all — try the Low degree instance above.
  • Otherwise the search can ignore low-degree candidates entirely.

7Why you can trust the output

The pleasant feature of this whole approach is that it is self-certifying. The algorithm does not ask you to believe its complexity analysis. It returns a payment system, and conditions (P1)–(P4) are checkable by direct arithmetic; by Theorem 3.2, any committee admitting such a system satisfies core+, and hence the core.

So a mistake in the truncation bound cannot produce a wrong committee. It would show up as an infeasible linear program or an exceeded round bound — a loud failure, not a silent one. That is worth more than it sounds: it means the delicate part of the analysis is not load-bearing for correctness, only for speed.

what was checked here

The reference implementation was run on 2,728 instances — including exhaustive sweeps of every profile on 3 candidates with 3 voters and on 4 candidates with 2 voters, at every committee size — plus 200 instances built to defeat the degree preprocessing and force repair rounds. For every one, the returned certificate was re-checked against (P1)–(P4) in exact rational arithmetic, and the committee was independently confirmed to be in the ordinary core by exhaustive enumeration of all \(2^m-1\) proposals. No infeasible LP and no round-bound breach occurred.

The browser version above is checked the same way on every run: the pills under the stepper report the certificate check and an exhaustive core check of the committee it produced.

8Run it yourself

Everything above, on an instance of your own. Set the number of voters, candidates and seats, then drag candidates onto voters to build the approval profile. The panel underneath writes out the dual programme that profile induces — the payment variables and the four families of constraints — and the runner then walks the repair loop at whatever pace you choose.

The thing worth watching is the pills. \((P1)\), \((P2)\) and \((P4)\) stay satisfied from the very first step, because every payment system the algorithm ever touches is feasible for them. Only \((P3)\) is in doubt, and the whole loop exists to repair it. The moment every loser drops below the quota, the payment system in hand is the certificate — and the last panel takes that certificate back to the core by brute force, checking all \(2^m-1\) proposals independently of anything the algorithm claimed.
